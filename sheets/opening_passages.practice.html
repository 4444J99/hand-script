Canonical Opening Passages — Multi-Script Handwriting Sheet

Passages: 8 Relation Discipline: cognate != translation != borrowing != comparison
Methodological Note: Each passage pairs script-appropriate ruling geometry (four-line, right-to-left abjad, Shirorekha head-line, or Tianzige square grid) with typed lexical links so inherited cognates are never conflated with Septuagint/Vulgate translation choices or script borrowings.
Homer, Iliad 1.1 (Greek · alphabet)
ILIAD.1.1
Μῆνιν ἄειδε θεὰ Πηληϊάδεω Ἀχιλῆος
Transliteration: Mēnin aeide thea Pēlēïadeō Akhilēos
Gloss: Sing, goddess, the wrath of Peleus' son Achilles
Handwriting Focus: Polytonic Greek epic hexameter opening: note circumflex on Μῆνιν, diaeresis on Πηληϊάδεω, and word-final sigma ς.
  • cognate *ped- (proto-indo-european) → πούς (ποδός) (ancient-greek): Inherited Hellenic o-grade nominative pous, oblique stem pod-.
  • translation דָּבָר (hebrew) → λόγος (ancient-greek): Septuagint renders Hebrew dabar ('word/speech/matter') with Greek logos.
  • translation λόγος (ancient-greek) → verbum (latin): Vulgate narrows polysemous Greek logos ('speech/account/reason') into Latin verbum ('word').
  • interpretation λόγος (ancient-greek) → 道 (classical-chinese): Chinese biblical translators chose 道 (dao) to carry the cosmic/ordering register of Johannine logos alongside its Classical sense 'to speak'.
Ovid, Metamorphoses 1.1-2 (Latin · alphabet)
METAMORPHOSES.1.1
In nova fert animus mutatas dicere formas corpora
Transliteration: In nova fert animus mutatas dicere formas corpora
Gloss: My mind leads me to speak of forms changed into new bodies
Handwriting Focus: Classical Latin baseline hand; anchors the transformation lineage cited in HS-F001.
  • cognate *ped- (proto-indo-european) → pēs (pedis) (latin): Inherited Italic e-grade nominative pes, oblique stem ped-.
  • comparison γράφω (ancient-greek) → scrībō (latin): Parallel semantic shift ('scratch/incise' -> 'write') on distinct PIE roots (*gerbh- vs *skreybh-).
Genesis 1:1 (Masoretic Hebrew) (Hebrew (Square & Cursive note) · abjad)
GENESIS.1.1
בְּרֵאשִׁית בָּרָא אֱלֹהִים אֵת הַשָּׁמַיִם וְאֵת הָאָרֶץ
Transliteration: Bereshit bara Elohim et hashamayim ve'et ha'aretz
Gloss: In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth
Handwriting Focus: Right-to-left Ktav Ashuri practice: observe final mem (ם) in אֱלֹהִים / הַשָּׁמַיִם and final tsadi (ץ) in הָאָרֶץ.
  • cognate *š-l-m (proto-semitic) → שָׁלוֹם (hebrew): Northwest Semitic reflex (*š > sh, Canaanite shift *ā > ō).
  • translation שָׁלוֹם (hebrew) → εἰρήνη (ancient-greek): Standard Greek translation choice for Hebrew shalom; unrelated Indo-European etymology.
  • translation שָׁלוֹם (hebrew) → σωτηρία (ancient-greek): Shows translation is passage-sensitive rather than a mechanical 1-to-1 lexical substitution.
  • translation דָּבָר (hebrew) → λόγος (ancient-greek): Septuagint renders Hebrew dabar ('word/speech/matter') with Greek logos.
Quran 1:1 (Surah Al-Fatiha) (Arabic (Naskh study baseline) · abjad)
FATIHA.1.1
بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ
Transliteration: Bismi llahi r-rahmani r-rahim
Gloss: In the name of God, the Entirely Merciful, the Especially Merciful
Handwriting Focus: Right-to-left connected rasm practice: note shared trilateral root ر-ح-م (r-h-m) across الرَّحْمَٰنِ and الرَّحِيمِ, and non-connecting ر.
  • cognate *š-l-m (proto-semitic) → سَلَام (arabic): Central Semitic reflex (*š > s, preserved long ā).
  • word_formation كَتَبَ (arabic) → كِتَاب (arabic): Fi'al nominal template on root ك-ت-ب yielding kitab ('book/writing').
  • comparison كِتَاب (arabic) → 書 (classical-chinese): Contrasts consonantal template derivation with brush-depicting morphograph 書.
Dao De Jing 1 (Han Characters / Japanese Kanji · morphographic)
DAODEJING.1.1
道可道非常道名可名非常名
Transliteration: Dao ke dao fei chang dao; ming ke ming fei chang ming
Gloss: The Way that can be spoken is not the constant Way; the name that can be named is not the constant name
Handwriting Focus: Tianzige square-grid character balance: observe 道 functioning sequentially as noun ('Way') and verb ('to speak').
  • interpretation λόγος (ancient-greek) → 道 (classical-chinese): Chinese biblical translators chose 道 (dao) to carry the cosmic/ordering register of Johannine logos alongside its Classical sense 'to speak'.
  • word_formation 木 (classical-chinese) → 本 (classical-chinese): Indicative (zhishi) stroke added to base of 木 to denote 'root/origin'.
  • word_formation 木 (classical-chinese) → 林 (classical-chinese): Compound ideograph (huiyi) pairing two 木 graphs for 'grove/woods'.
Rigveda 1.1.1 (Devanagari · abugida)
RIGVEDA.1.1.1
अग्निमीळे पुरोहितं यज्ञस्य देवमृत्विजम्
Transliteration: agnim ile purohitam yajnasya devam rtvijam
Gloss: I praise Agni, the household priest, the divine minister of the sacrifice
Handwriting Focus: Devanagari head-line (shirorekha) practice: note pre-base placement of short-i matra (ि) in ग्नि and ृ vocalic-r hook below द.
  • cognate *ped- (proto-indo-european) → पद् (sanskrit): Inherited Indo-Iranian reflex preserving initial *p- and dental stop.
Slovo o polku Igoreve (opening question, modernized Cyrillic) (Cyrillic (Russian inventory) · alphabet)
SLOVO.OPENING
Не лепо ли ны бяшеть братие начати старыми словесы
Transliteration: Ne lepo li ny byashet bratie nachati starymi slovesy
Gloss: Might it not suit us, brothers, to begin in the old words
Handwriting Focus: Cyrillic upright stem spacing across л, п, н, ш, т, м, ы; keep print forms distinct from connected cursive.
  • cognate *ped- (proto-indo-european) → пеший (russian): Inherited Balto-Slavic derivative (*ped-sjo- > peshij 'on foot').
  • comparison пеший (russian) → нога (russian): Lexical replacement within Slavic: noga descends from *noga ('claw/unguis'), not *ped-.
Hunminjeongeum Eonhae (opening clause) (Hangul · featural)
HUNMIN.PREFACE
나랏말싸미중국에달아문자와로서르사맛디아니할쌔
Transliteration: Narat malssami Junggug-e dara munja-waro seoreu samatdi anihalssae

Six-Step Notebook Session Protocol (15-20 min baseline):
1. warm up (15%): Write baseline strokes, curves, or radicals slowly with focus on proportion.
2. copying (25%): Copy a short canonical line or glyph sequence attentively onto physical paper.
3. memory rewrite (20%): Cover the exemplar and rewrite the target sequence from visual-motor recall.
4. observation (15%): Annotate recurring graphemes, diacritics, positional allographs, or roots.
5. composition (15%): Write one original variation, word recombination, or script adaptation.
6. reflection (10%): Record one concise note on friction, legibility, or sound-sign mapping.
